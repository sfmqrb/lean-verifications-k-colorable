# Paper results and their Lean counterparts

This file maps the paper "An n^{1/O(log* n)} Lower Bound for Online Coloring of k-Colorable Graphs"
to the Lean library `OnlineColouring` (`OnlineColouring/*.lean`). There is one table
for each of Sections 1 and 4 to 10 and Appendices A to D, with one row for every numbered definition, construction,
observation, lemma, theorem, corollary, proposition, remark and table, and for the values that the paper states in
running text. Section 2 (overview) and Section 3 (related work) have no numbered statements: Section 2 explains
results of the later sections on examples, and Section 3 relates them to the literature. What they add of their own
(the plays traced in the figures, the dictionary between blow-ups and arc graphs, the remark of Section 3 on
presentations that stay connected) is listed in the section "Not formalized".

How to read the tables.

- All Lean names are relative to the namespace `OnlineColouring`: `main_logStar_three` is
  `OnlineColouring.main_logStar_three`, and `Blow.theorem_6_4` is `OnlineColouring.Blow.theorem_6_4`.
- The third column gives the declarations that state the paper item; the fourth column gives the files in which they
  are found. The last column lists other forms of the statement, hypotheses that the formal statement adds or drops,
  and other deviations. Where it says nothing about deviations, the formal statement has the quantifiers, hypotheses
  and constants of the paper.
- "Deterministic online algorithm" always means a proper one (`OnlineAlg.IsProper`); `docs/library.md` explains why this
  and the other conventions of the library (colours and indices from `0`, discrete distributions) change nothing.
- An item marked "not formalized" has no formal counterpart; these items are collected in the section "Not
  formalized".

`AxiomCheck.lean` prints the axioms of every declaration named in this file. All of them depend only on `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, or on no axiom.

## Where the formal proof takes a different route from the paper

The statements are those of the paper; the following proofs are organised differently.

| Result | Paper | Lean |
|---|---|---|
| Observation 4.3 | Zermelo's theorem for the game in which colours are renamed in the order of their first use. | Backward induction over the number of remaining rounds, written out (`Det.exists_presents`); colours need not be renamed. For "an adversary gives an input", the adversary is played against an algorithm that first deletes from every neighbour set the indices that are not earlier vertices (`Det.exists_input_of_hist`). |
| Theorem 1.4, first sentence | Through an adversary of size `N` for `k′`-colourable graphs, `j` reservation blow-ups below it, and Observation 4.3. | Through inputs: "every proper algorithm uses `K` colours on some input" (`ChiDetAtLeast`, `ForcesWith`), with the same construction and sizes and without the stop rule (`chiDetAtLeast_transfer`). `Paper.transfer_chiDet` takes `j = lg* k′ + 1`; `transfer_least` has the paper's least `j`. |
| Theorem 1.4, second sentence | Through `N_{k′}(K) = O(K^{1/ε})`. | Directly with `M = ⌊n^{1/e}⌋` and padding (`transfer_poly_real`). |
| Theorem 1.5, second sentence | The least `λ` with `card P_λ(k) ≥ c`; a bound for the number of vertices of `G`, then a comparison with `n`; threshold `tow(max(7, lg* k + 3)) + 1`. | The size bound at `i = min(j, lg* c + 1)`, directly for `n`. This gives `c^(lg* n - lg* k - 3) ≤ n`, with subtraction in `ℕ`, whenever `lg* n ≥ 8`, for every `k` (`IsFullTowerAnc.firstFit_pow_le`, `IsPaddedTower.firstFit_pow_le`). The statement with the real exponent, `exists_threshold_le_rpow`, is derived from it with the paper's threshold `tow(max(7, lg* k + 3)) + 1`, above which the exponent `lg* n - lg* k - 3` is positive. |
| Proposition 9.4 | The down-sets are numbered in the order of their first appearance. | A fixed numbering of `J*(P)`; the algorithm is patched so that it is proper on every history (`labelAlg`). |
